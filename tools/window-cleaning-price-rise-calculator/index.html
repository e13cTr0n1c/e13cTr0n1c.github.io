How it works

We work out how many times a year each customer is cleaned (52 divided by the weeks between cleans), multiply by the price, and compare the old and new totals. The customers you could lose is how many could leave before the higher prices from everyone else stop covering them.

  • New price: your average price plus the rise, rounded up to the next 10p, 50p or £1 if you pick one.
  • Takings a year: customers × price × cleans a year.
  • Customers you could lose: customers × (1 take away old price ÷ new price), rounded down.

Tips

  • Tell customers in writing a few weeks before the new price starts.
  • Rises often land better at the start of spring than in the middle of winter.
  • Keep a note of who you told and when. The round book has a Told on column for this.

Questions

How much should I put my window cleaning prices up?

Many cleaners review prices once a year in line with costs. This calculator shows what any percentage adds so you can choose.

How many customers can I lose after a price rise?

Enter your round and the rise; the calculator shows how many could leave before you take less than now.

Do I round prices up?

Most rounds use tidy prices. Pick 10p, 50p or £1 and the calculator rounds each new price up.
