Can I still claim working from home tax relief?

For employees who had to work from home. I check the GOV.UK tests, show when each old year closes, and estimate the tax relief. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: you can claim only if you had to work from home, not if you chose to. The flat amount is £6 a week. At basic rate that is £1.20 a week, or £62.40 over 52 weeks. 2022/23 closes on 5 April 2027. There is no employee claim for 2026/27, from 6 April 2026. This is an estimate, not tax advice. What to do next

  1. Claim online. GOV.UK has a service that checks working from home and starts the claim. Start the working from home claim. The guide is on GOV.UK: working from home.
  2. Or use form P87 by post if your total expenses claim for that tax year is £2,500 or less. More than £2,500 has to go on a Self Assessment return. The £2,500 is the whole year's job expenses, not only working from home. GOV.UK: claim by post.
  3. If you already send a Self Assessment return, claim on that return. Do not send the same costs online or on form P87.
  4. Keep the evidence. For £6 a week in 2022/23 or a later year you still claim, send something that shows you had to work from home, such as your contract. For the exact amount, also send copies of receipts or bills. Keep a copy of what you send.
  5. Do not claim if the costs were paid back in full. If your employer, or anyone else, paid all of the costs, there is nothing to claim. If they paid part, claim only the part they did not pay.

How I work it out

  1. The test. I only add up a claim if you tick a GOV.UK example that can qualify: no office, or the job needs you to live far away. Choosing to work from home, a contract that lets you, or a full office, stops the claim.
  2. £6 a week. I multiply £6 by the weeks. If you leave the weeks at 52, a year is £312.00. Then I multiply by your tax rate. 20% of £312.00 is £62.40, which is the GOV.UK example of £1.20 a week for 52 weeks.
  3. Exact costs. I use the pounds you enter instead of £6 a week, then the same tax rate. That route needs receipts or bills.
  4. Money already paid back. I take that off before the tax rate. If nothing is left, the relief is £0.
  5. The deadline. Each tax year ends on 5 April. The claim closes on 5 April four years later. 2022/23 closes on 5 April 2027. I count calendar days from today. The closing day itself is still in time.

The rules I follow

  • Who qualifies, before 6 April 2026. The duties at home have to be substantive, they need facilities, those facilities are not available at the employer's premises (or you live too far away to travel in each day), and you could not choose the office instead. The two GOV.UK examples sit inside that test. EIM32760.
  • What the relief is on. Extra household costs for the work, such as business phone calls and gas and electricity for the work area. Not rent or broadband you also use privately. Either £6 a week, or the exact amount. GOV.UK: working from home.
  • From 6 April 2026. Employees cannot deduct unreimbursed household expenses. Section 360B ITEPA 2003 stops a deduction under section 336. It was inserted by the Finance Act 2026 for 2026/27 and later years. Travel costs are a different rule. EIM32759 and section 360B.
  • Employer payments carry on. An employer can still pay £6 a week, or £26 a month, with no bills. That £6 is not a maximum. They can pay more if they keep evidence of the extra cost. The exemption is section 316A, and it is not the same as you claiming relief. EIM01476.
  • How you claim. Online, or form P87 when the year's total expenses claim is £2,500 or less, or your Self Assessment return if you send one or the year is over £2,500. You need to have paid tax in the year. GOV.UK: claim tax relief for job expenses.

What I am not sure about

  • 52 weeks. A tax year is 52 weeks and 1 day, or 52 weeks and 2 days in 2023/24 because that year includes 29 February 2024. GOV.UK states the relief per week, not as a yearly total. I default to 52. The government impact note rounded a full year to £62 at basic rate and £124 at higher rate. I keep the pence: £62.40 and £124.80. GOV.UK: removal of the relief.
  • The tax you already paid. Relief cannot be more than the income tax you paid that year. I do not know your tax, so a high exact cost at a high rate can come out above the refund you would actually get.
  • The £2,500 line. I only compare the working from home figure on this page. Other job expenses in the same year count towards it.
  • A pass here is not a decision. Ticking "no office" or "live far away" follows the GOV.UK examples. EIM32760 still wants substantive duties, facilities, and no choice. HMRC decides the claim.
  • One sentence on the GOV.UK page still says you can claim for this tax year and the four previous tax years. The line at the top of that same page says employees cannot claim for 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. I follow the specific line.
  • Older pages talked about claiming because of coronavirus, and about £4 a week before 6 April 2020. The page I checked on 16 November 2026 does not. I do not calculate years before 2022/23.
  • Scottish rates. I do not store them. Type the percentage you pay. Bands move, and your rate is the one that applies to this slice of income.

Questions I get asked

Who can claim working from home tax relief?

You can claim only if you had to work from home. GOV.UK gives two examples: your employer does not have an office, or your job requires you to live far away from the office. You cannot claim if you chose to work from home. That includes a contract that lets you work from home some or all of the time, and an office you cannot go to sometimes because it is full.

GOV.UK: working from home

How much is £6 a week worth?

GOV.UK says basic rate tax relief on £6 a week is £1.20 a week. I use 52 weeks unless you enter a different number. At 20% that is £62.40 for a full year. At 40% it is £124.80. At 45% it is £140.40. If you pay Scottish Income Tax, enter the rate you pay. This is an estimate, not tax advice.

Can I claim for 2026/27?

No. From the tax year 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027, employees cannot claim tax relief for working from home. You can still claim for the four previous tax years if you are in time. Your employer can still pay you for extra household costs without deducting tax, including £6 a week with no bills, or more if they keep evidence.

EIM32759

When do the older years close?

A claim must be within 4 years from the end of the tax year. 2022/23 closes on 5 April 2027. 2023/24 closes on 5 April 2028. 2024/25 closes on 5 April 2029. 2025/26 closes on 5 April 2030.

GOV.UK: claim by post

How do I claim, and what if I was paid back?

Use the GOV.UK online service for working from home. If your total expenses claim for a year is £2,500 or less you can send form P87 by post. If it is more than £2,500 you need a Self Assessment return. If you already send a Self Assessment return, claim on that return. If your employer or anyone else paid all of the costs back, you cannot claim. If they paid some, you claim only the part they did not pay. Keep evidence that you had to work from home. For the exact amount, also keep receipts or bills.

What can I not include?

Only costs that are for your work, such as business phone calls and gas and electricity for your work area. You cannot claim things you use privately as well, such as rent or broadband.
