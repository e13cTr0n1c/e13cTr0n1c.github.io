Energy price cap January 2027: how much will my energy bill go up?

I compare the Ofgem price cap in force from 1 October to 31 December 2026 with a forecast for 1 January to 31 March 2027. The energy price cap January 2027 is not published yet, so the January side is a forecast. Put in your own use, or pick a typical home, and see the yearly and monthly change. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

FORECAST

This is a forecast, not the official cap. Official figures are due from Ofgem on 25 November 2026.

Quick answer: a typical home using 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas, paying by Direct Debit at the Great Britain average, comes to £1,723.61 a year now and £2,165.89 a year on the January forecast. That is up £442.28 a year, about £36.86 a month, or 25.7%. Ofgem's published typical figure for the current cap is £1,723. Cornwall Insight's published typical forecast is £2,166. Both of those are rounded to the nearest pound.

Before you use the figure

  • This is an estimate, not financial advice.
  • The cap limits unit rates and standing charges on a default tariff. It does not limit your total bill.
  • What you pay still depends on your tariff, your meter and how much energy you use. A fixed tariff is outside the cap.
  • Gas prices include VAT at 5%. Electricity has no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Those rates already include that, so I do not add VAT on top.
  • It covers England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland is regulated separately.
  • I use single rate electricity. Economy 7 and other multi rate meters are not in this sum.

How I turn the cap into a bill

For each fuel I do: yearly kWh times the unit rate, plus 365 times the daily standing charge. I then add electricity and gas. The monthly figure is that year divided by 12. The cap runs for three months at a time, but Ofgem describes it as a yearly bill at typical use, so I do the same.

At the Great Britain Direct Debit average, typical use is 2,500 times 26.32p plus 365 times 54.83p for electricity (£858.13), and 9,500 times 7.97p plus 365 times 29.68p for gas (£865.48). Together that is £1,723.61. Ofgem publishes this as £1,723, rounded to the nearest pound.

Current cap, 1 October to 31 December 2026

These are Ofgem's published unit rates and standing charges. For Direct Debit across Great Britain, electricity is 26.32p per kWh with a standing charge of 54.83p a day, and gas is 7.97p per kWh with a standing charge of 29.68p a day. Standard credit and prepayment differ, and so does each region. The Great Britain average is the one Ofgem publishes. It is not a simple average of the 14 regions.

Regional rates come from Ofgem's cap tables for this period. Those tables are exclusive of VAT. I leave electricity as published, because VAT on electricity is 0% in this period, and I add 5% to gas, then round to 2 decimal places. That matches the pence rates Ofgem shows for households.

Sources, last checked 24 November 2026: Ofgem, unit rates and standing charges, Ofgem cap tables PDF, 26 August 2026, Ofgem announcement, 26 August 2026.

January to March 2027 is a FORECAST

Ofgem has not published the January cap yet. Cornwall Insight's final forecast, published on 18 November 2026, says a typical dual fuel Direct Debit bill would be £2,166 a year, up £443 on the current £1,723. Their table totals £2,165.89, which they round to £2,166. Selectra reports the same forecast and the same rates. I checked both on 24 November 2026.

They call the rise 26%. £443 divided by £1,723 is 25.7%, which rounds to 26%. A flat 26% of £1,723 would be about £2,171, which is not the figure they published. I do not add 26% to every rate. Their table raises the electricity unit rate from 26.32p to 32.54p and the gas unit rate from 7.97p to 10.90p. Standing charges barely move. They show them as 56p and 31p a day, rounded to the nearest penny.

I scale each current rate by the ratio of that forecast to today's Great Britain Direct Debit rate. Electricity and gas are scaled separately, and the unit rate is scaled separately from the standing charge. For standing charges I use the daily rate implied by their yearly fuel costs (55.78p and 31.04p), because 56p and 31p would not reproduce the £1,017.09 of electricity and £1,148.80 of gas in their table. On the typical Direct Debit average, that scale lands on £2,165.89. I apply the same scales to every region and payment method, because the forecast was published only for the Direct Debit average. Prepayment and standard credit in January are therefore an estimate built from that forecast.

Cornwall Insight says the removal of VAT from electricity is included in the forecast, and that the VAT cut has been extended to July 2027. Ofgem's published position for the current cap is that electricity has no VAT from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. The £100 Household Energy Payment is not part of the cap, so it is not taken off these figures. The plan to remove the gas standing charge is not in this January forecast either.

Sources, last checked 24 November 2026: Cornwall Insight, 18 November 2026, Selectra, 18 November 2026.

What typical use means

From 1 July 2026, Ofgem's typical domestic consumption values for a single rate meter are 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas. Low is 1,600 kWh and 6,000 kWh. High is 3,800 kWh and 14,000 kWh. Until 30 June 2026 the typical figures were 2,700 kWh of electricity and 11,500 kWh of gas. If an older bill or article still quotes 2,700 and 11,500, that is the previous typical home, not the one Ofgem uses for the cap now.

Source, last checked 24 November 2026: Ofgem, Review of typical domestic consumption values, decision, 27 May 2026.

Questions I get asked

When is the January 2027 price cap announced?

Ofgem says the cap for 1 January 2027 to 31 March 2027 will be announced by 25 November 2026. Until then, the January side of this calculator is Cornwall Insight's forecast, not the official cap.

Last checked 24 November 2026. Source: Ofgem, Changes to the energy price cap between 1 October and 31 December 2026.

How much will my energy bill go up?

Cornwall Insight's final forecast, published on 18 November 2026, puts a typical dual fuel Direct Debit bill at £2,166 a year. That is £443 more than Ofgem's current typical figure of £1,723. They describe the rise as 26%. £443 divided by £1,723 is 25.7%, which rounds to 26%. Your own change depends on how much energy you use, where you live and how you pay. The cap does not set a fixed bill.

Last checked 24 November 2026. Sources: Cornwall Insight and Ofgem.

Does the energy price cap limit my total bill?

No. Ofgem caps the unit rate and the daily standing charge on a default tariff. It does not cap the total you pay. If you use more energy, you pay more. A fixed tariff is outside the cap.

Last checked 24 November 2026. Source: Ofgem.

What about prepayment meters?

The cap covers prepayment meters. For 1 October to 31 December 2026, Ofgem's typical prepayment bill is £1,678 a year, against £1,723 for Direct Debit and £1,861 if you pay by standard credit. Prepayment standing charges match Direct Debit and the unit rates are lower. Cornwall Insight published its January forecast for Direct Debit. This page applies the same rate changes to prepayment, so the January prepayment figure is an estimate built from that forecast, not a separate prepayment forecast.

Last checked 24 November 2026. Source: Ofgem summary of the October to December 2026 cap.

What is typical energy usage?

From 1 July 2026, Ofgem's typical domestic use is 2,500 kWh of electricity and 9,500 kWh of gas a year on a single rate meter. Low use is 1,600 kWh of electricity and 6,000 kWh of gas. High use is 3,800 kWh of electricity and 14,000 kWh of gas. Until 30 June 2026 the typical figures were 2,700 kWh of electricity and 11,500 kWh of gas.

Last checked 24 November 2026. Source: Ofgem TDCV decision, 27 May 2026.
