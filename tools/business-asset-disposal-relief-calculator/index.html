Business Asset Disposal Relief: sell before or after 6 April 2027?

For anyone selling all or part of a business, or shares in their personal company, who may qualify for Business Asset Disposal Relief. Put in your gain, the relief you have already used and your sale date, and I show the Capital Gains Tax under the £1 million limit and under the new £500,000 limit, and the difference. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: Budget 2026 cuts the Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit from £1 million to £500,000 for qualifying disposals on or after 6 April 2027. Gains within the limit are taxed at 18% in both 2026 to 2027 and 2027 to 2028. The rest of the gain is taxed at the normal rates of 18% within your basic rate band and 24% above it. Sources: GOV.UK: new BADR lifetime limit, GOV.UK Budget 2026 rates and allowances.

Budget 2026 figures, still to be legislated The new limit was announced at Budget 2026 and will be legislated in the Finance Bill. If you have signed but not completed before 6 April 2027

For Capital Gains Tax, a sale normally happens on the day the contract becomes unconditional, even if completion comes later. That would let someone sign an unconditional contract before 6 April 2027, complete afterwards and keep the £1 million limit. The government is closing that route.

If you enter into an unconditional contract before 6 April 2027 and it completes on or after that date, your gain is held to the £500,000 limit unless you can show one of these:

  • you and the buyer did not enter into the contract with a purpose of getting a tax advantage from the timing rule, or
  • if you and the buyer are connected, the contract was made for wholly commercial reasons.

If you rely on either of those and your gain is over £100,000, you must also make a statement saying so. A separate rule covers share reorganisations: if you elect on or after 6 April 2027 to switch off the normal share reorganisation rules so you can claim the relief, the disposal is treated as happening when you make the election, so the £500,000 limit applies.

Source: GOV.UK: Capital Gains Tax, new Business Asset Disposal Relief lifetime limit, Proposed revisions.

The figures I use

  • Lifetime limit: £1 million for disposals before 6 April 2027, £500,000 on or after. Relief claimed in the past counts towards it. GOV.UK policy paper, Annex A.
  • Rate on gains getting the relief: 18% in 2026 to 2027 and in 2027 to 2028. GOV.UK: Business Asset Disposal Relief, Annex A.
  • Normal Capital Gains Tax rates: 18% within the basic rate band and 24% above it, in both years. GOV.UK: Capital Gains Tax rates, Annex A.
  • Annual exempt amount £3,000 and basic rate band £37,700 in both years. Annex A.
  • Gains getting the relief use up any basic rate band before your other gains do. To be confirmed I could not find this on the GOV.UK pages I checked, so treat it with care if your income is low.

A worked check

A £800,000 gain, no relief used before and income above the basic rate band. Before 6 April 2027 the whole gain gets the relief: (£800,000 less £3,000) at 18% is £143,460. On or after that date £500,000 gets the relief at 18%, which is £90,000, and the other £300,000 less £3,000 is taxed at 24%, which is £71,280. That is £161,280, or £17,820 more.

Figures checked on GOV.UK on 29 October 2026.
