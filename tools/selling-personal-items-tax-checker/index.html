Do I pay tax selling my old stuff?

For a clear out on Vinted, eBay or anywhere else. I check whether you are selling your own used things, or buying and making items to sell, and I say which tax GOV.UK points at. It is a selling personal items tax check. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: your own personal possessions sold for £6,000 or less each are not Capital Gains Tax and you do not report that sale. Over £6,000, tax may be due on the gain, and the gain can be capped. A car, and most things with a predictable life of 50 years or less, are usually exempt. Buying to resell, or making items to sell, is likely to be trading, with a £1,000 allowance until 5 April 2027 and £3,000 from 6 April 2027. A platform report after 30 sales or about £1,700 is not a tax by itself. This is an estimate, not tax advice. Sources: GOV.UK: personal possessions, GOV.UK: income from online platforms.

    How I work it out

    1. Own things, or a trade. If you buy to resell, or you make items to sell, I stop at trading. GOV.UK says that is likely to be Income Tax, with the trading allowance. I do not then run the Capital Gains Tax sums.
    2. Cars and wasting assets. Your car is exempt unless you used it for business. A wasting asset is generally exempt unless capital allowances come into it. I ask that before I look at the price.
    3. £6,000 or less. If the proceeds did not exceed £6,000, I do not calculate a gain. HS293 says that sale does not go on the return as a gain.
    4. Over £6,000. Tax may be due on the gain. When the proceeds were not more than £15,000, I show the maximum chargeable gain: 5/3 of the amount over £6,000. Your actual gain, if it is lower, is the one that counts.
    5. A loss. Selling for less than you paid means there is no gain, so there is no Capital Gains Tax. A loss you might claim is a separate rule, and it is restricted below £6,000.

    A worked check from GOV.UK

    HS293 sells an antique mirror for £7,500. It cost £1,500. Costs of the sale were £250.

    • £7,500 minus £6,000 is £1,500. Five thirds of £1,500 is £2,500. That is the maximum chargeable gain.
    • The actual gain is £7,500, minus £250, minus £1,500, which is £5,750.
    • The lower figure is £2,500. That is the chargeable gain.
    • The annual exempt amount for 2026 to 2027 is £3,000. This one gain is not above that allowance. Other gains in the same tax year still count. I do not work out an 18% or 24% bill from this page, because I do not know the rest of your income.

    The rules I follow

    • Personal possessions of £6,000 or less. You do not need to calculate a gain if the disposal proceeds did not exceed £6,000. You only include a gain on your tax return where the proceeds were more than £6,000 and the possession is not exempt. Clearing a loft, with nothing worth more than £6,000, is GOV.UK's example of a sale you are unlikely to report. HS293 and GOV.UK: online platforms.
    • Over £6,000, and the 5/3 cap. If the proceeds exceeded £6,000 but were not more than £15,000, the chargeable gain is the lower of your actual gain and 5/3 of the amount over £6,000. Above £15,000 you use the actual gain. HS293, section 2.
    • The annual exempt amount. You only pay Capital Gains Tax if your overall gains for the year, after losses and reliefs, are above the annual exempt amount. For an individual that is £3,000 in 2025 to 2026 and £3,000 in 2026 to 2027. From 6 April 2026 the rates for an individual are 18% and 24%. GOV.UK: rates and allowances and GOV.UK: Capital Gains Tax rates.
    • Sets. The £6,000 limit applies to a set, not to each piece, when the pieces are similar and complementary and worth more together. If you break a set and sell the parts to the same person, to people acting together, or to connected people, the limit applies to the set as a whole. GOV.UK: personal possessions.
    • A share. If you own a possession with other people, you are exempt on the first £6,000 of your share. Enter your share in the price box. GOV.UK: personal possessions.
    • Cars. You do not pay Capital Gains Tax on your car, unless you have used it for business. GOV.UK: personal possessions.
    • Wasting assets. A wasting asset has a predictable life of 50 years or less. Plant and machinery always count. A gain or loss is exempt unless you have, or could have, claimed capital allowances because you used it in a business. Loaning jewellery, art or an antique to a business to use as plant can also take a gain out of the exemption. HS293, parts 4 and 5.
    • Selling for less. No gain means no Capital Gains Tax. If you claim a loss and the proceeds were less than £6,000, you recalculate it as if you sold for £6,000. At £6,000 or more the loss is not restricted that way. You cannot claim a loss on a private car or, in most cases, a wasting asset. HS293, section 2.3.
    • Trading. You are probably trading if you bought goods intending to sell them for a profit, or you made them, including as a hobby. The trading allowance is £1,000 a tax year. From 6 April 2027 it rises to £3,000, and the reporting threshold rises with it. The badges of trade are weighed together. GOV.UK: online platforms, GOV.UK: trading allowance, GOV.UK: increasing the trading allowance, BIM20205 badges of trade.
    • The 30 sales rule. Platforms collect details and can report them to HMRC. For goods, you are not reported when you make fewer than 30 sales in a calendar year and receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700). The report is not a tax. GOV.UK: selling on a digital platform.

    Where the GOV.UK pages are not identical

    • £6,000 or more, or more than £6,000. The short personal possessions page says you may pay Capital Gains Tax if you sell for £6,000 or more and you make a gain. HS293 says you do not calculate a gain if the proceeds did not exceed £6,000, and you only include a gain where the proceeds were more than £6,000. The Capital Gains Manual says there is no chargeable gain if the consideration does not exceed £6,000. I follow HS293 and the manual. A sale of exactly £6,000 is not over the line.
    • The £15,000 line. HS293 runs the 5/3 steps when proceeds were not more than £15,000, and the normal gain when they were more than £15,000. The manual says that at £15,000 or more the 5/3 maximum is higher than the actual gain, so the relief does not reduce it. At exactly £15,000, 5/3 of £9,000 is £15,000, which is not lower than a real gain on a sale of that price. I show the cap up to and including £15,000. I do not show one above that.
    • 30 sales and 2,000 euros. The seller page says you are not reported if you make fewer than 30 sales of goods and receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700). The operator page says the exception is 2,000 euros or less for fewer than 30 sales of goods, and that it does not apply to services, renting transport, or renting property. At exactly 2,000 euros the two pages do not use the same words. I quote both. Either way, the report is not a tax.
    • The trading allowance pages. The allowances guidance, last updated on 8 May 2019, still says £1,000. The policy paper of 28 October 2026 and Budget 2026 OOTLAR section 1.5 say the allowance and the reporting threshold rise to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. I use £1,000 up to 5 April 2027 and £3,000 from 6 April 2027. Both are on GOV.UK.
    • A loss is not a tax bill. Selling for less than you paid means there is no gain, so there is nothing to pay on this sale. HS293 still restricts a loss below £6,000. I say so, rather than treating "no tax" as a full loss you can claim.

    Checked on 27 November 2026. HS293 is written for the tax year 2025 to 2026. The £6,000 limit and the 5/3 steps are the rules it sets out. The £3,000 annual exempt amount is on the rates and allowances page for both 2025 to 2026 and 2026 to 2027.

    Questions I get asked

    Do I pay tax selling on Vinted?

    Selling your own used things on Vinted is unlikely to be Income Tax. GOV.UK says you are unlikely to pay tax when you sell personal items from your home, such as the contents of a loft or a garage. If each item, or each set you normally sell together, goes for £6,000 or less, you do not calculate a Capital Gains Tax gain and you do not include it on your tax return. If you buy items to resell, or you make items to sell, that is likely to be trading. This is an estimate, not tax advice.

    GOV.UK: income from online platforms

    Is there tax on eBay sales in the UK?

    The same rules apply on eBay as on any other sale. Your own personal possessions sold for £6,000 or less each are outside Capital Gains Tax. A sale over £6,000 can lead to Capital Gains Tax on the gain, after the annual exempt amount of £3,000 for 2026 to 2027. Buying to resell, or making items to sell, is looked at as trading, with the £1,000 trading allowance until 5 April 2027 and £3,000 from 6 April 2027. This is an estimate, not tax advice.

    GOV.UK: personal possessions

    What is the HMRC Vinted 30 sales rule?

    From 1 January 2024, digital platforms can report sellers to HMRC. Your details are not reported if you make fewer than 30 sales of goods in a calendar year and you receive less than 2,000 euros (about £1,700) for those sales. The report is data sharing. GOV.UK says it does not automatically mean you owe tax. It does not turn a clear out of your own things into a business.

    GOV.UK: selling on a digital platform

    Do I pay tax when I sell personal items?

    Personal possessions are things you own for your own use, such as clothes, furniture, jewellery, computers and phones. You probably do not pay Income Tax on selling them. Capital Gains Tax comes in only if you sell a possession for more than £6,000 and you make a gain, and the possession is not exempt. Cars are exempt unless used for business. Wasting assets, with a predictable life of 50 years or less, are generally exempt unless used in a business.

    What if one item sells for more than £6,000?

    You may have to pay Capital Gains Tax on the gain. If the proceeds were not more than £15,000, the chargeable gain cannot be more than 5/3 of the amount over £6,000. The chargeable gain is the lower of that cap and your actual gain. You only pay tax if your gains for the year are above the £3,000 annual exempt amount for 2026 to 2027. A set counts as one item for the £6,000 limit. Selling for less than you paid means there is no gain.

    I buy clothes to resell. Which allowance do I use?

    GOV.UK says you are probably trading if you bought goods intending to sell them for a profit, or you made them, including as a hobby. The trading allowance is £1,000 up to 5 April 2027 and £3,000 from 6 April 2027. My side hustle checker compares that allowance with your costs. HMRC also looks at the badges of trade, such as how often you sell and whether you bought the items to make a profit.

    GOV.UK: increasing the trading allowance
