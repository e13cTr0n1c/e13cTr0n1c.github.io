How much interest will HMRC add if I pay tax late?

Put in the tax, the day it was due and the day you paid it (or today, if you have not paid yet). I work out HMRC late payment interest day by day, and I split it wherever the rate changed. You can switch to repayment interest, which is what HMRC pays when they repay you late. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: late payment interest is 7.75% from 9 January 2026 and 8% from 24 November 2026. Repayment interest is 2.75%, then 3%. It is simple interest: the amount times the rate, divided by 365, times the days. A £1,000 bill due on 31 January 2025 and paid on 31 January 2026 comes to £79.59 of late payment interest, across six rate changes. Source: GOV.UK: HMRC interest rates.

How I work it out

  1. Count the days. HMRC's manual counts the gap between the start date and the end date. Due on 31 January and paid on 1 February is 1 day. Paid on the due date is none. The days run from the day after the due date up to and including the day you pay.
  2. Pick the rate for each day. If HMRC's rate changes while the tax is unpaid, I split the period on the change date and use the rate in force on each day.
  3. Simple interest. Tax times the rate, divided by 365, times the days in that stretch. I divide by 365 even when the stretch covers 29 February. Interest is not charged on interest.
  4. Add the stretches and round once to the nearest penny.

A worked check

£1,000 due on 31 January 2025 and paid on 31 January 2026 is 365 days and six rates: 24 days at 7.25%, 40 days at 7%, 52 days at 8.5%, 91 days at 8.25%, 135 days at 8% and 23 days at 7.75%. Late payment interest is £79.59. The same dates on repayment interest come to £32.22.

Day count: HMRC Compliance Handbook CH140220 and CH141120. Simple interest: CH140260.

The rates I use

Late payment interest is the Bank of England base rate plus 4% from 6 April 2025. Before that it was the base rate plus 2.5%. Repayment interest is the base rate minus 1%, and it cannot fall below 0.5% (the minimum floor). I store the published percentages, so a future base rate change does nothing here until I add the row HMRC publishes.

The Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November 2026. HMRC published the new rates on 6 November 2026: 8% late payment interest and 3% repayment interest from 24 November 2026 for these taxes. I use that published date. I do not ask you to guess a start date, and I do not apply 8% before 24 November 2026.

Questions I get asked

How does HMRC work out late payment interest?

HMRC charges simple interest for each day the tax is late. The number of days is the gap between the due date and the payment date, so tax due on 31 January and paid on 1 February is one day. If the rate changes, each stretch is worked out on its own and then added up.

Source: CH141120

What is the rate now?

From 9 January 2026 the late payment rate is 7.75% and the repayment rate is 2.75%. From 24 November 2026 late payment interest is 8% and repayment interest is 3%. Those November rates are published. They are not a guess.

Source: GOV.UK: HMRC interest rates

Is this the same as interest on a late invoice?

No. This page is the interest HMRC charges you, or pays you. Interest you can add to a late business invoice is a different rule: 8% plus the Bank of England base rate for a half year, and it stays fixed for that debt. I keep that on its own calculator.

Source: GOV.UK: interest on late commercial payments

Does paying some of it help?

Yes. Interest runs on the amount still unpaid. This calculator uses one amount for the whole period. If you paid in stages, run each stage with its own amount and dates, or use the figure on your HMRC statement.
