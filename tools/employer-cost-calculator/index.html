What does it really cost to employ someone?

Put in an hourly rate and the hours a week, and I add the employer National Insurance and the workplace pension on top of the wage, so you can see the yearly, monthly and hourly cost to you as the employer. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: The National Living Wage for workers aged 21 and over is £12.71 an hour now and £13.21 from April 2027. Employers pay National Insurance at 15% on pay above £96 a week (£5,000 a year), and the Employment Allowance can take up to £10,500 a year off that bill. Sources: GOV.UK minimum wage rates, GOV.UK Budget 2026 OOTLAR annex A.

An estimate, not advice I look at one employee paid the same hourly rate for 52 weeks, with employer National Insurance worked out on the yearly threshold. Real payroll runs week by week or month by month, so your figures will differ a little. This is an estimate, not tax, legal or financial advice.

Is a full time minimum wage worker £28.3k a year?

I have seen it reported that a full time worker on the minimum wage costs an employer about £28,300 a year. I have not found that figure on GOV.UK, so treat it as a media claim. Here is what my sums give with the default settings: 37.5 hours a week, 3% pension and no Employment Allowance.

CaseWageTotal cost
£12.71, 37.5 hours, 2026 to 2027£24,784.50£28,308.51
£13.21, 37.5 hours, from April 2027£25,759.50£29,459.01
£13.21, 40 hours, from April 2027£27,476.80£31,485.42

So the £28.3k figure matches today's £12.71 rate for a 37.5 hour week. On the April 2027 rate the same worker costs about £29,500, and more on a 40 hour week. A small firm that can use the Employment Allowance would pay no employer National Insurance on one such worker.

The figures I use

  • National Living Wage, aged 21 and over: £12.71 from April 2026 and £13.21 from April 2027. GOV.UK minimum wage rates.
  • Employer National Insurance: 15% above the secondary threshold of £96 a week in both 2026 to 2027 and 2027 to 2028. Budget 2026 OOTLAR annex A, Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027.
  • Secondary threshold as a yearly figure: £5,000 for 2026 to 2027. Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027. For 2027 to 2028 I also use £5,000. To be confirmed Annex A gives only the weekly £96, so the yearly figure is my assumption until HMRC publishes its 2027 to 2028 employer rates.
  • Employment Allowance: up to £10,500 a year for eligible employers in both years. Budget 2026 OOTLAR annex A, GOV.UK Employment Allowance, who can claim.
  • Pension: 3% employer minimum on qualifying earnings between £6,240 and £50,270, with the £10,000 earnings trigger, for 2026 to 2027. DWP review of the auto enrolment thresholds for 2026/27. For 2027 to 2028 I keep the same band. To be confirmed The government reviews these each year and I could not find the 2027 to 2028 figures on GOV.UK.
  • 5.6 weeks' paid holiday a year, used only for the cost per hour actually worked. GOV.UK holiday entitlement.

A worked check

£12.71 for 37.5 hours over 52 weeks is £24,784.50. Employer National Insurance is 15% of £19,784.50, the pay above £5,000, which is £2,967.68. The pension is 3% of £18,544.50, the pay above £6,240, which is £556.33. That makes £28,308.51 a year, £2,359.04 a month and £14.52 per paid hour.

Figures checked on GOV.UK on 30 October 2026.
