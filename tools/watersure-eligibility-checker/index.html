Can I get WaterSure?

For a metered home in England or Wales. I check the benefit, the children and the medical tests published for WaterSure. Nothing you tick leaves your browser. Your water company still decides.

Quick answer: WaterSure caps a metered water and sewerage bill at the company's average household bill. It is for a home where someone gets a qualifying benefit, and either there is Child Benefit for three or more children under 19 who live there, or someone has a medical condition that means they have to use a lot more water. It is not a fixed payment. The £428 figure is what GOV.UK said on 27 November 2026 households could get off their bills. That page does not call it an average, and I do not treat it as your saving.

General guidance, not advice I follow the WaterSure regulations, the GOV.UK pages, CCW and Ofwat. I do not know your bill, your award letters, or what your company will accept. A pass here is not a decision. If the bill matters, contact your water company.

1 · Where is the home?

2 · Is the home on a water meter?

3 · Does someone in the home get a qualifying benefit?

The regulations list these. CCW's public FAQ names only Universal Credit, Housing Benefit and Pension Credit. I use the longer legal list. Pick the one that applies. If you get more than one, pick any that is on the list.

The regulations count Child Tax Credit only if it includes the individual element.

4 · Children under 19, or a medical condition?

You need one of these as well as the benefit. A child who is already 19 does not count. The children test in the regulations is Child Benefit, not just the number of children in the home.

The person who gets the benefit or tax credit has to be the person entitled to the Child Benefit.

The regulations name seven conditions, and they also allow another condition if it means a significant extra amount of water.

CCW gives eczema, psoriasis and varicose ulceration as examples of weeping skin disease. The regulations just say weeping skin disease.

This condition does not count if a health authority pays towards the water used for dialysis in the billing period.

The condition has to be the reason for a significant extra amount of water. Watering a garden or filling a pool does not count as this.

5 · Is any of the water used for a garden or a pool?

The regulations say the supply must not be used to water a garden, other than by hand, with any apparatus, or to automatically refill a pond or a swimming pool bigger than 10,000 litres.

    What WaterSure does

    WaterSure caps the water and sewerage charges for a qualifying household at the company's average charges for household consumers. If the metered bill for that period would have been lower than the average, the regulations say you pay the lower amount. You are not pushed up to the average.

    GOV.UK said on 27 November 2026 that WaterSure already supports around 300,000 households, that fewer than half of eligible households are claiming, and that companies will use Department for Work and Pensions data to contact up to 250,000 more households who may already qualify. The same page says households could get £428 a year off their water bills. That figure is not a fixed payment. The page does not call £428 an average. A quote on that page from CCW says just one in four households are aware of the scheme. Ofwat says just under 282,300 household customers were on WaterSure in 2025-26, saving around £413 each on average, from a sector total of just under £116.5 million. GOV.UK said on 6 March 2026 that the average reduction in 2024/25 was £325, and that about 260,000 households received a discount that year, about 230,000 in England and 30,000 in Wales.

    The rules I follow

    • Where. The regulations are marked England and Wales. Ofwat says they do not apply to companies whose areas are wholly or mainly in Wales, and that Dŵr Cymru and Hafren Dyfrdwy offer WaterSure voluntarily. CCW says the same about the two Welsh companies. I do not cover Scotland or Northern Ireland, because those pages do not.
    • Meter. The regulations require charges fixed by volume, or charges that would be fixed by volume if you were not already getting the help. GOV.UK's current eligibility also includes waiting for a meter, and an assessed charge if a meter cannot be fitted. Ofwat includes a meter or waiting for one. I treat a fitted meter, and waiting for one, as meeting that test, and I ask you to check an assessed charge with the company.
    • Benefit. Housing Benefit, Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, State Pension Credit, Child Tax Credit if it includes the individual element, Working Tax Credit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, or Universal Credit. New style JSA and new style ESA are not those benefits. Council Tax Reduction is not on the list.
    • Children. The same person has to be entitled to Child Benefit for three or more children under the age of 19 who live in the home. CCW says this. GOV.UK's 6 March 2026 summary says three or more children under 19 living at home and does not repeat Child Benefit. Ofwat's 2025-26 report says three or more children under 19 living at home in full-time education. I do not add a full-time education tick. I follow the Child Benefit test. A 19 year old does not count.
    • Medical. Desquamation, weeping skin disease, incontinence, abdominal stoma, Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, or renal failure requiring dialysis at home, where that condition means a significant extra amount of water. Another diagnosed condition can qualify on the same water test. Home dialysis does not count if a health authority contributes to the cost of the water used for dialysis in the billing period.
    • Garden and pool. The water must not be used to water a garden, other than by hand, by means of any apparatus, or to automatically replenish a pond or a swimming pool with a capacity greater than 10,000 litres. CCW says you cannot apply if the high use is from garden sprinklers or swimming pools.
    • Home. The consumer has to occupy the premises as their only or principal home. Another use must not be the principal use.
    • Apply and renew. You apply and the company checks it. In England the application goes to the water company. Where that company's area is wholly or mainly in Wales, it goes to the sewerage company. The help lasts 12 months, or to the end of the billing period if the 12 months finish part way through. GOV.UK says apply annually. A company can meet the legal duty through a social tariff that cuts the bill by at least as much, so the help might not be labelled WaterSure.

    Checked on 28 November 2026. Regulations, regulation 2, regulation 3, regulation 4, regulation 5. CCW: What is WaterSure?

    What is not in force yet

    The 27 November 2026 announcement does not change who qualifies. The written statement says companies will use DWP data to identify households in England and Wales, contact them, and help them apply. If nothing has changed, someone already eligible will be re-enrolled rather than having to reapply. You can still contact the company yourself. I do not know when a company will write to you, or what that letter will look like.

    A separate reform was set out by GOV.UK on 6 March 2026. It is not in force on 28 November 2026. That page says it is set to come into force in early 2027, mandatory in England and voluntary in Wales. It would add Disability Living Allowance, Attendance Allowance and Personal Independence Payment, still only for a high water user for a medical reason, and only if household income is below £25,745. GOV.UK says that brings in about 53,000 more households. The same page says companies have agreed voluntarily that people will no longer need to pay for a doctor's note. It also describes a future cap at the lower of the overall average and the metered average, and a household of one person capped at the average bill for one person. I do not apply those cap rules, because they are not the law I am reading today.

    There are currently no known outstanding effects on regulations 2 to 5 of the 2015 regulations, on the legislation.gov.uk pages I read.

    Checked on 28 November 2026. Written statement HCWS512, 27 November 2026. GOV.UK, 6 March 2026.

    How to apply

    1. Contact your water company. That is the route on the CCW page. In England the regulations say the application goes to the water company. In Wales, where the water company's area is wholly or mainly in Wales, the regulations say it goes to the sewerage company. Ask for WaterSure, or for the equivalent scheme if you are in Wales.
    2. Have the notices ready. The company may refuse the application without a notice of entitlement to the benefit or tax credit. For a benefit, that notice has to be dated no more than 12 months before you apply. For a tax credit, no more than 6 months. For the children route, they can ask for the Child Benefit notice for three or more children under 19 who live there.
    3. Medical information. For a listed condition they can ask how it increases water use. For a condition that is not on the list, the regulations allow them to ask for a certificate signed by a registered medical practitioner. GOV.UK says companies have agreed voluntarily that you should not have to pay for a doctor's note. Ask your company what they still want.
    4. Renew it. You have to apply again. The company should remind you before the 12 months, or the billing period, runs out.

    CCW's lines, from its WaterSure page, are 0300 034 2222 for customers in England and 0300 034 3333 for customers in Wales. Those lines are CCW, not your water company, and they are not an application form.

    Checked on 28 November 2026.

    Questions I get asked

    Can I get WaterSure?

    You may, if the home is in England or Wales, the supply is on a water meter or you are waiting for one, someone in the home gets a qualifying benefit or tax credit, and the home has either Child Benefit for three or more children under 19 who live there, or a medical condition that means someone has to use a significant extra amount of water. Your water company decides the application. In Wales the scheme is voluntary for Dŵr Cymru and Hafren Dyfrdwy. This is general guidance, not advice.

    Checked on 28 November 2026. Regulation 2. CCW.

    Who qualifies for WaterSure?

    The current regulations list Housing Benefit, Income Support, income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, State Pension Credit, Child Tax Credit if it includes the individual element, Working Tax Credit, income-related Employment and Support Allowance, and Universal Credit. The same person must be entitled to Child Benefit for three or more children under 19 who live in the home, or someone who lives there must have a listed medical condition, or another condition, that means they have to use a significant extra amount of water. CCW's FAQ names only Universal Credit, Housing Benefit and Pension Credit. Personal Independence Payment, Disability Living Allowance and Attendance Allowance are not on the current list. GOV.UK says adding them is a reform set for early 2027, with a household income below £25,745, and it is not in force.

    Checked on 28 November 2026. Regulation 2. GOV.UK, 6 March 2026.

    How much does WaterSure save?

    WaterSure caps the bill at the company's average household bill. If your metered bill would have been lower, you pay the lower amount. It is not a fixed payment. GOV.UK said on 27 November 2026 that households could get £428 a year off their water bills. That page does not call £428 an average. Ofwat says the average saving in 2025-26 was around £413. GOV.UK said the average reduction in 2024/25 was £325.

    Checked on 28 November 2026. GOV.UK, 27 November 2026. Ofwat, 2025-26. Regulation 3.

    How do I apply?

    Contact your water company. In England the regulations say you apply to the water company. Where the water company's area is wholly or mainly in Wales, the regulations say you apply to the sewerage company. CCW says contact your water company, including for the equivalent scheme in Wales. The help lasts 12 months, or to the end of the billing period, and you renew it. CCW's lines are 0300 034 2222 in England and 0300 034 3333 in Wales.
