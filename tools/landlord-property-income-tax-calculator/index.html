Landlord property income tax calculator (UK, 2027)

For landlords who own rental property in their own name in England, Wales or Northern Ireland. Put in your other income and your rental profit and see the income tax on your property income in 2026 to 2027 and from 6 April 2027, when Budget 2026 brings in separate property rates. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: from 6 April 2027 property income is taxed at 24% basic rate, 44% higher rate and 49% additional rate. In 2026 to 2027 it is taxed at the main rates of 20%, 40% and 45%. Source: GOV.UK Budget 2026 rates and allowances.

Budget 2026 figures, still to be legislated The property rates will be legislated in Finance Bill 2026 to 2027. The rate of the mortgage interest tax credit from April 2027 is to be confirmed; I assume it follows the 24% property basic rate. Scotland and Wales can set their own property rates. This is an estimate, not advice. It is the extra income tax your rental profit adds, after the Personal Allowance and taper over £100,000. I keep the finance cost credit simple: the rate times the lower of your finance costs and your rental profit. Savings, dividends, losses brought forward and the property allowance are not included.

The figures I use

  • 2026 to 2027: property income taxed at the main rates, 20%, 40% and 45%.
  • 2027 to 2028: property basic rate 24%, higher rate 44%, additional rate 49%.
  • Personal Allowance £12,570, basic rate band £37,700, additional rate over £125,140, in both years.
  • Finance cost credit 20% in 2026 to 2027. The 2027 to 2028 rate is to be confirmed.

Sources: GOV.UK: Budget 2026 Annex A, rates and allowances, GOV.UK: Budget 2026 OOTLAR, section 1.2, GOV.UK: tax relief for residential landlords.

A worked check

Other income of £30,000 and rental profit of £10,000 with £3,000 of mortgage interest: all the rent falls in the basic rate band. Tax is £2,000 less a £600 credit, so £1,400 in 2026 to 2027, and £2,400 less a £720 credit, so £1,680 in 2027 to 2028.
