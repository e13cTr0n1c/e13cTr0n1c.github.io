Questions

How will the Budget affect my take home pay?

Put your salary into the calculator. The left column uses the confirmed 2026/27 rules and the right column uses the Budget figures. The difference shows what you gain or lose a year and a month.

When is the Budget 2026?

The Chancellor delivered the Budget on Wednesday 28 October 2026. I added the confirmed figures to this calculator the same day, from the Budget documents on GOV.UK.

Why is the difference £0?

Because the Budget does not change tax on pay. For 2027/28 the Personal Allowance stays at £12,570, the Income Tax rates and bands stay at 20%, 40% and 45%, and employee National Insurance stays at 8% and 2%. The Budget does raise the Income Tax rates on savings and property income to 24%, 44% and 49% from 6 April 2027, but that is not pay from a job, so this calculator leaves it out.

Do Budget changes apply straight away?

Usually not. Most Income Tax and National Insurance changes start on 6 April, the start of a new tax year. This Budget has no mid year changes to tax on pay. The after the Budget column is for 6 April 2027 to 5 April 2028.

Which rates does the calculator use now?

For 2026/27: a Personal Allowance of £12,570, basic rate 20% to £50,270, higher rate 40% to £125,140 and additional rate 45% above that. Employee National Insurance is 8% between £12,570 and £50,270 and 2% above. Scotland has its own Income Tax bands.

Is this financial advice?

No. It is an estimate for an employee paid through PAYE with a standard tax code. Your payslip and HMRC's own figures are the final word.
