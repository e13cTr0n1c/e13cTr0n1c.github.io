Pension Tax Allowance checker (2027/28)

For anyone over State Pension age from 6 April 2027. I built this to see how the new Pension Tax Allowance works on real mixes of income. Put in what you get from each source and I show your Income Tax with and without the allowance, your marginal rate and what an extra £100 of pension really leaves you. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: from 6 April 2027, if you are over State Pension age and your total income from all sources is £13,037 or less, the Pension Tax Allowance wipes out up to £94 of Income Tax, so someone on the full new State Pension of £13,036.40 and nothing else pays nothing. Above £13,037 it is withdrawn at £1 for every £3 of extra income and it is gone at £13,319. Sources: GOV.UK Pension Tax Allowance explainer and GOV.UK Budget 2026 Annex A.

How the Pension Tax Allowance works

  • Who gets it: people above State Pension age, or who reach it during the tax year. It is based on total income from any source, so you do not need to be getting a State Pension.
  • Full allowance: total income of £13,037 or less in 2027/28. The full new State Pension is £13,036.40, so on that alone the £93.28 of tax you would otherwise owe is wiped out. GOV.UK says the full allowance is worth up to £94.
  • Taper: above £13,037 the allowance falls by £1 for every £3 of extra income. At £13,319 it is gone.
  • The 53% band: between £13,037 and £13,319 each extra £1 of pension or pay costs 20p in basic rate tax plus about 33p of lost allowance, so you keep roughly 47p. Extra savings interest or rent can cost more, because those are taxed at 24% from April 2027.
  • Later years: it runs for 2027/28, 2028/29 and 2029/30 and the thresholds rise in line with the full new State Pension. The 2028/29 and 2029/30 thresholds are to be confirmed.
  • Nothing to claim: HMRC applies it automatically. It does not change your Personal Allowance, it cannot be transferred to a partner and it never pays out a refund.

The other figures I use for 2027/28

  • Personal Allowance £12,570, basic rate band £37,700, additional rate over £125,140.
  • Pay and pensions: 20%, 40%, 45%. Property income: 24%, 44%, 49%. Savings interest: 24%, 44%, 49%. Dividends: 10.75%, 35.75%, 39.35%.
  • Starting rate for savings 0% on up to £5,000, Personal Savings Allowance £1,000 basic and £500 higher, Dividend Allowance £500.
  • I set the Personal Allowance against pay and pensions first, then property, then savings, then dividends. How HMRC orders property income against the allowance from April 2027 is to be confirmed.

Worked checks against the GOV.UK examples

  • Full new State Pension only, £13,036.40: tax without the allowance £93.28, with it £0.
  • Partial State Pension plus a small private pension, £12,970: tax £80 without, £0 with.
  • Pay and rent of £13,186.40: allowance £94 minus a third of £149.40, so £44.20. GOV.UK rounds this to £44.
  • Private pension and savings of £13,400: above £13,319, so no allowance and £166 of tax on GOV.UK's figures.
