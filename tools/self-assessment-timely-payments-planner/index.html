Self Assessment timely payments planner (2029)

For anyone who makes Payments on Account. From April 2029 these move to smaller, automated payments during the tax year. I built this to see what that does to my cashflow, especially in the 2029/30 transition year. Put in last year's bill and how your income falls across the year. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: from April 2029, if you make Payments on Account you will pay during the tax year by automated payments such as Direct Debit, or through PAYE if you opt in and have enough PAYE income. Payments are still based on last year's tax and your return is still due by 31 January. 2029/30 is a transition year where the last payment for 2028/29 overlaps with the new payments. How often you pay has not been decided, so I show both monthly and quarterly. Sources: GOV.UK timely payments factsheet and GOV.UK summary of consultation responses.

The new schedule is not final HMRC says it will set out the frequency and timing of in-year payments, safeguards and how the transition works in 2027. This is an estimate on a schedule that is not final. I assume monthly payments at the end of each month from April 2029, quarterly payments at the end of June, September, December and March, that the 31 July 2029 payment and 31 January 2030 balancing payment for 2028/29 are still due, and that this year's bill matches last year's. All of these are to be confirmed by HMRC.

What GOV.UK has confirmed

  • From April 2029 people who make Payments on Account will make smaller, more regular payments during the tax year they earn the income.
  • Automated by default, for example by Direct Debit. If you have enough PAYE income from a job or a private pension you can choose to pay through PAYE instead.
  • Still based on last year's tax, and you will be able to update it online if your circumstances change.
  • Returns are still due by 31 January. GOV.UK does not yet say when any balancing payment will fall under the new system, so I keep it on 31 January as to be confirmed.
  • 2029/30 is a transition year, because the final payment for 2028/29 overlaps with the new in-year payments. The detail of the transition is still to be confirmed.
  • Frequency is not decided. GOV.UK does not commit to monthly or quarterly. In the consultation, people open to change mostly preferred quarterly over monthly.
  • Who is affected: you make Payments on Account unless last year's bill was under £1,000 or more than 80% of your tax was taken at source, for example through your tax code.
  • Help before the switch: HMRC plans tools from 2028 to estimate your bill, track it and make voluntary payments early.

Why the transition year costs more

Today, in the year from April 2029 you would pay the second payment for 2028/29 in July 2029 and then, in January 2030, any balancing payment plus the first payment for 2029/30. That is one year's bill. Under the new rules the July and January payments for 2028/29 still fall in that year, and the whole of the 2029/30 bill is paid during the year. That brings forward half a year's bill.

A worked check

Last year's bill of £6,000 and a flat bill: today you pay £3,000 in July 2029 and £3,000 in January 2030, so £6,000 in the year. Monthly, you pay £500 a month plus the £3,000 July payment, so £9,000. Quarterly, you pay £1,500 a quarter plus £3,000 in July, also £9,000. The extra cash is £3,000. If two thirds of your income comes in from October to March, monthly or quarterly payments need a buffer of £1,800 in a normal year, because you pay before the busy months arrive.
