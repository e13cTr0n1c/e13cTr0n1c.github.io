Can we claim Marriage Allowance, and what is it worth?

If one of you earns under the Personal Allowance and the other is a basic rate taxpayer, you can move £1,260 of allowance across. I built this to check eligibility, the real saving after any tax the lower earner pays, and what backdating adds. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: Marriage Allowance moves £1,260 of Personal Allowance to your spouse or civil partner, cutting their tax by up to £252 a year. The lower earner normally earns under £12,570 and the other pays basic rate tax, usually £12,571 to £50,270 (£43,662 in Scotland). You can backdate to 6 April 2022. Sources: GOV.UK: Marriage Allowance, Budget 2026 OOTLAR Annex A.

Simple incomes only I assume your incomes are from wages, pensions, self employment or rent. If either of you has dividends, savings interest or benefits from a job, GOV.UK says to call the Income Tax helpline. This is an estimate, not tax or financial advice.

Things to know

  • The lower earner applies. The allowance then rolls over each year until you cancel it, for example if your income changes or your relationship ends. GOV.UK.
  • Living together is not enough. You must be married or in a civil partnership. GOV.UK.
  • Born before 6 April 1935? Married Couple's Allowance may be worth more, and you cannot have both. GOV.UK.
  • Child Benefit. If the higher earner is over £60,000 they cannot get Marriage Allowance anyway, but the High Income Child Benefit Charge may apply. My Child Benefit charge calculator works that out.

The figures I use

  • Transfer £1,260, cutting the partner's tax by up to £252 a year. Same in 2026/27 and 2027/28. GOV.UK, Budget 2026 OOTLAR Annex A.
  • Personal Allowance £12,570 in 2025/26 and 2026/27. Basic rate band £37,700. GOV.UK rates and allowances.
  • Partner's income usually £12,571 to £50,270, or £12,571 to £43,662 in Scotland. GOV.UK.
  • Backdating to 6 April 2022, the 2022/23 tax year. GOV.UK.
  • Backdated years at £252 each. To be confirmed I assume the £1,260 transfer and your incomes were the same in each earlier year. GOV.UK says the saving depends on each year's Personal Allowance rate.

A worked check

GOV.UK's example: the lower earner has £11,500 and the partner £20,000. The partner's tax falls by £252, the lower earner now pays tax on £190, which is £38, so the couple saves £214.
