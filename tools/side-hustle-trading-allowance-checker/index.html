Do I need to tell HMRC about my side hustle?

For anyone selling crafts, 3D prints or digital downloads, or doing odd jobs on the side. Put in what you took in and what it cost you, pick a tax year, and I show whether the trading allowance covers it and, if not, whether the flat allowance or your actual costs gives the lower taxable profit. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: Budget 2026 raises the trading allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 from 6 April 2027, for 2027 to 2028 onwards. It covers trading and miscellaneous income, and the Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income also rises to £3,000. Sources: GOV.UK: Increasing the trading allowance, GOV.UK Budget 2026 OOTLAR section 1.5.

Things to know

  • It is per person. The allowance is for individuals, so if you and your partner each run your own side hustle you each get one. GOV.UK: tax free allowances.
  • Some income cannot use it. You cannot use it on income from a company you or someone connected to you owns or controls, from a partnership where you or someone connected to you is a partner, or from your employer or your spouse's or civil partner's employer. GOV.UK: when you cannot use the allowances.
  • Platforms still share your data. Sites like eBay, Etsy and Vinted send HMRC your details and takings once you make 30 or more sales of goods, or get about £1,700 (2,000 euros), in a calendar year. That does not by itself mean you owe tax, and selling your own unwanted things is unlikely to be taxed. GOV.UK: selling on a digital platform.
  • Allowance or costs, not both. If you take the flat allowance you cannot also deduct your actual costs. GOV.UK: tax free allowances.
  • Other reasons to file. If you already send a tax return for another reason, you still fill it in. This checker only looks at your side income.

The figures I use

  • Trading allowance £1,000 in 2026 to 2027, £3,000 from 6 April 2027 for 2027 to 2028 onwards. GOV.UK policy paper, OOTLAR section 1.5.
  • The allowance covers trading and miscellaneous income, now and after the rise. GOV.UK policy paper, OOTLAR section 1.5.
  • Self Assessment reporting threshold for trading income: over £1,000 now, rising to £3,000 from 6 April 2027. GOV.UK: tax free allowances, OOTLAR section 1.5.
  • Reporting for miscellaneous (other) income from 2027 to 2028. To be confirmed Today other income from £1,000 to £2,500 means contacting HMRC and over £2,500 means Self Assessment. The Budget papers name the reporting rise for trading income only, so I could not confirm what happens for miscellaneous income.
  • Property allowance stays at £1,000. To be confirmed The Budget papers I checked do not mention a change, but I could not find a GOV.UK page that says it stays the same in 2027 to 2028. GOV.UK: property allowance.

A worked check

3D prints bringing in £4,500 with £1,900 of costs. In 2026 to 2027 the flat allowance gives £4,500 less £1,000, which is £3,500, while actual costs give £2,600, so costs win. In 2027 to 2028 the allowance gives £1,500, which beats £2,600, so the allowance wins and saves £1,100 of taxable profit. Crafts bringing in £2,400 need reporting in 2026 to 2027 but are fully covered from 2027 to 2028.
