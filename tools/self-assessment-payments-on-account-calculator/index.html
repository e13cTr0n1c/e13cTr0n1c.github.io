How much do I pay HMRC on 31 January and 31 July?

Payments on account catch a lot of people out, especially the first time the January bill is one and a half times what you expected. Put in your Self Assessment bill and I show the balancing payment, both payments on account, and what changes if you ask HMRC to reduce them. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Quick answer: each payment on account is half of last year's Self Assessment bill, due by 31 January and 31 July. You do not make them if last year's bill was under £1,000, or if more than 80% of your tax was paid outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code. Any balancing payment is due by 31 January too. Source: GOV.UK: Payments on account.

Be careful. If you reduce them and your bill turns out higher, HMRC charges interest on the difference. You can reduce them online in your HMRC account or by sending form SA303. GOV.UK: Reduce your payments on account. Interest rates: GOV.UK: HMRC interest rates.

The figures I use

  • Each payment on account is half of last year's Self Assessment bill, including Class 4 National Insurance. Due by midnight on 31 January and 31 July. GOV.UK.
  • No payments on account if last year's bill was under £1,000, or if you paid more than 80% of the tax you owed outside Self Assessment. GOV.UK.
  • The balancing payment is your bill minus the payments on account you made, due by 31 January after the tax year. It also includes Capital Gains Tax and student loan repayments, which payments on account do not cover. GOV.UK.
  • You can reduce payments on account online or with form SA303, and pay interest if you reduce them too far. GOV.UK.
  • For a 2025/26 bill the dates are 31 January 2027 (balancing payment plus 1st payment for 2026/27) and 31 July 2027 (2nd payment). For a 2024/25 bill they are 31 January 2026 and 31 July 2026.
  • How HMRC rounds each half. To be confirmed I round each payment down to the penny. HMRC's own figure may differ by a penny.

A worked check

GOV.UK's own example: a £3,000 bill with £1,800 already paid on account. The balancing payment is £1,200 and the 1st payment on account is £1,500, so £2,700 by 31 January, then £1,500 by 31 July. A first return of £3,000 with nothing paid on account would mean £4,500 in January.
