MTD for landlords with jointly owned property

If you own a rental with someone else, only your share counts, and you get some choice about expenses in your updates.

Checked on 2 November 2026

A guide, not advice. I wrote this from GOV.UK guidance checked on 2 November 2026. It is a guide and an estimate, not tax advice. HMRC decides, so check anything that matters with HMRC or an accountant.

Questions and answers

Does the whole rent count towards my qualifying income?

No. Only your share counts. GOV.UK gives a property owned with a sibling that brings in £50,000, shared equally, so each owner has qualifying income of £25,000 if they have no self employment income.

Source: GOV.UK: Work out your qualifying income for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

What if I only hear my share after expenses?

GOV.UK says if you only get notice of your share after expenses have been taken off, HMRC assesses that figure as your qualifying income.

Source: GOV.UK: Work out your qualifying income for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax

What goes in my quarterly updates for a jointly let property?

You can include either income and expenses, or income only. If you leave expenses out, you must report them after the tax year by resending your fourth quarterly update before you submit your tax return.

Source: GOV.UK: Send quarterly updates

What if I also own properties on my own?

For properties you own solely, you must include both income and expenses in your quarterly updates.

Source: GOV.UK: Send quarterly updates

Which thresholds apply?

The same as everyone: over £50,000 from 6 April 2026, over £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and over £20,000 from 6 April 2028, each judged on an earlier tax year.

Source: GOV.UK: Find out if and when you need to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax
